In short

What is legal intake?

Legal intake is the process a law firm uses to respond to a potential client, collect the facts of their matter, check whether the firm can take it and move them to a signed agreement. A strong intake process is fast, consistent and tracked, so no inquiry waits hours for a call and every outcome is recorded.

Where intake breaks

Why law firms lose good cases at intake

Most firms do not have a lead problem as much as a response problem. An injured person who fills in a form often contacts more than one firm. The firm that responds first, asks the right questions and makes the next step easy usually gets the consultation.

These are the four leaks we see most often. Each one raises your cost per signed case, even when your ads are working.

Slow first response

Inquiries wait in an inbox or voicemail for hours, while another firm calls within minutes.

No screening

Intake staff spend time on people the firm cannot help, and miss the ones it can.

No routing

Leads arrive by email, phone and forms with no single place that shows who called whom.

No outcome tracking

Nobody can say which ad, case type or source produced the cases that actually signed.

The intake process

A law firm intake process step by step

This is the intake flow we build behind every lead campaign. Your team stays in control of the conversation and the decision to take a case. The system makes sure nothing is missed between the ad and the call.

  1. 01

    Screening form

    The prospect answers short questions about what happened, when, injuries, treatment and whether they already have a lawyer.

  2. 02

    Phone verification

    The number is verified, for example with a one-time code, so intake is not calling fake or mistyped numbers.

  3. 03

    Instant response

    An automatic text and email go out within seconds, confirming the inquiry and setting expectations for the call.

  4. 04

    Routing

    The lead lands in your CRM, tagged by case type, and your intake team is alerted right away.

  5. 05

    First call

    Your team calls with the screening answers in front of them, so the first conversation starts in the middle, not at the beginning.

  6. 06

    Follow-up and tracking

    Unreached leads get a follow-up sequence, and every lead is marked as signed, declined or lost, with the reason.

Speed to lead

Why speed matters in legal intake

When someone has just been in an accident or hurt at work, they are often comparing firms the same day. A text that arrives within seconds tells them the firm is responsive. A call within minutes, while they still remember filling in the form, is far more likely to connect than a call the next morning.

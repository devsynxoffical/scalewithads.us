How to Find Hidden Facebook Interests Your Competitors Aren't Targeting

A practical workflow for finding hidden Facebook interests in Ads Manager, building tighter interest stacks and testing them without wasting budget.

Most advertisers in a niche open Ads Manager, type the same obvious keyword into detailed targeting and pick the same handful of interests from the top of the list. That is why those audiences get expensive: everyone in the category is bidding on them. "Hidden" Facebook interests are simply the relevant interests that sit one or two steps away from the obvious ones, where fewer competitors are looking.

This guide walks through the workflow our team uses to find them, filter out the irrelevant ones and test them properly. It's the written companion to our exclusive MVA leads campaigns, which use the same process for personal injury cases.

What counts as a hidden Facebook interest?

Meta does not publish a list of "hidden" interests. The term describes interests that are real, still available in detailed targeting, and relevant to your buyer, but that rarely show up when you search for your niche's main keyword. They usually fall into a few groups:

  • Publications, podcasts, creators and communities your buyers follow.
  • Tools, software and brands your buyers use in their work or daily life.
  • Job titles, industries and employers that line up with your customer profile.
  • Life situations and adjacent hobbies that correlate with the need you solve.

A note on what's no longer available

Meta retired the standalone Audience Insights tool in 2021 and removed many sensitive detailed targeting options (for example, ones related to health, religion and political beliefs) in 2022. Targeting options also change over time, so treat any "interest list" you download as a starting point and confirm every interest still exists in Ads Manager.

Step 1: Start from your buyer, not from your keyword

Before opening Ads Manager, write down who actually buys from you. What do they read, which brands and tools do they use, what jobs do they do, and what was happening in their life right before they needed you? Twenty minutes with your CRM, sales-call notes and best customers' public profiles gives you far better seed ideas than the search box does.

Step 2: Mine Ads Manager's own suggestions

  1. 1In an ad set, open Detailed targeting and type one seed idea from your list.
  2. 2Add a relevant interest, then open the Suggestions tab. Meta shows related interests based on what you've already added.
  3. 3Follow the chain: add a good suggestion, check the new suggestions it unlocks, and note anything that fits your buyer.
  4. 4Use Browse to explore the category tree around each interest. Related options are often grouped next to each other.
  5. 5Repeat for every seed on your list, and keep a spreadsheet of every interest with its estimated audience size.

Several third-party "interest explorer" tools speed this up by querying Meta's Marketing API for interest suggestions in bulk. They surface the same interests Meta makes available, just faster, so they're useful for volume but don't unlock anything secret.

Step 3: Filter ruthlessly for relevance

A long list is easy; a useful list takes judgement. For each interest, ask whether someone with that interest is meaningfully more likely to need your offer than the average person. Remove anything ambiguous: interests that share a name with something unrelated, very broad entertainment categories, and anything that only loosely connects to your buyer. Interests with a tiny estimated audience are also hard to spend on, so group them rather than testing them alone.

Step 4: Build themed interest stacks

Instead of one giant ad set with fifty interests, group interests into a few themes, for example "industry publications", "tools they use" and "life situation". Each theme becomes its own ad set. That way, when one performs, you know why, and you can expand that theme rather than guessing.

  • Keep each stack focused on one idea so results are readable.
  • Exclude existing customers and recent leads so you're paying for new people.
  • Watch audience overlap between stacks; heavy overlap makes the ad sets compete with each other.
  • If you use Advantage+ audience, remember Meta treats your interests as suggestions and may go beyond them, so compare against a test with that option off when you need a clean read.

Step 5: Test with the same creative and judge on lead quality

Run every stack with the same ads, so the only variable is the audience. Give each one enough budget to exit the learning phase before you decide. Then judge on what matters to the business, not just cost per click: cost per qualified lead, booked calls and closed revenue. A cheap audience that produces unqualified leads is not a win.

Step 6: Expand winners and refresh the list

When a stack works, go back to Step 2 with its best interests as new seeds; the suggestions they unlock are often just as strong. Revisit your list every month or two, because Meta adds, merges and removes options, and competitors eventually find the same pockets you did.

Example: personal injury campaigns

Personal injury is a crowded category on Meta, and most firms and agencies target the same legal and insurance interests. Key takeaways

  • Hidden interests are relevant interests one or two steps away from the obvious ones.
  • Start from a written buyer profile, then use Ads Manager's Suggestions and Browse to expand it.
  • Filter for genuine relevance, group interests into themes and test each theme with identical creative.
  • Judge audiences on qualified leads and revenue, not clicks, and refresh the list regularly.

Frequently asked questions

What are hidden Facebook interests?

They are real, relevant interests in Meta's detailed targeting that sit one or two steps away from the obvious ones for your niche, such as publications, tools, job titles and life situations your buyers share. Fewer competitors target them, so they are often less contested.

Is Facebook Audience Insights still available?

No. Meta retired the standalone Audience Insights tool in 2021 and removed many sensitive detailed targeting options in 2022. Use the Suggestions and Browse features in Ads Manager instead, and confirm every interest still exists before you use it.

How do you test Facebook interest stacks?

Group interests into a few themed ad sets, run each with the same creative so the audience is the only variable, give each enough budget to exit the learning phase, and judge them on qualified leads and revenue rather than cost per click.

Does Advantage+ audience ignore my interests?

Meta treats your interests as suggestions when Advantage+ audience is on and may show ads beyond them. For a clean read on an interest stack, compare it against a test with that option turned off.

Advertising rules

Pennsylvania lawyer advertising rules

Pennsylvania's Rules of Professional Conduct are among the more detailed in the country on advertising content. These points shape every Philadelphia campaign we run. This is a summary, not legal advice; your firm should review its ads against the full rules.

  • Rule 7.1: no misleading claims

    A lawyer may not make a false or misleading communication about the lawyer or their services. Reports of past results may be misleading if they create an unjustified expectation, and a disclaimer or qualifying language may prevent that.

  • Rule 7.2: endorsements and dramatizations

    Paid endorsements must disclose that the endorser is paid, celebrity or public figure endorsements are not allowed, and portrayals by non-clients or re-enactments must be disclosed as dramatizations.

  • Rule 7.2: fee advertising

    An ad that says there is no fee without recovery must disclose that the client will be liable for certain expenses, if that is the case. An advertised specific fee must be honored for at least 90 days.

  • Rule 7.2: office location and responsibility

    Ads must disclose the city or town of the office where the lawyers who will perform the services principally practice, and name at least one lawyer or firm responsible for the content.

  • Rule 7.2: specialty claims

    A lawyer who is not certified but claims a specialty based on experience must include a disclaimer that they are not certified, and may not claim specialization in more than one field.

  • Rule 7.3: solicitation

    No live person-to-person solicitation for pecuniary gain, and the comments say that includes text messages. Targeted direct mail is allowed, but not to people whose physical, emotional or mental state prevents reasonable judgment.

Source: Disciplinary Board of the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania, Rules of Professional Conduct . Because Rule 7.3 treats text messages as live person-to-person contact, our follow-up texts only go to people who asked to be contacted through your form. How we work

How we run campaigns for Philadelphia firms

We begin with your case types, the neighborhoods and suburbs you serve and your intake capacity. Then we write ads that follow Pennsylvania's content rules from the start: the responsible firm and office location are named, there are no celebrity endorsements, any dramatization is labeled and fee statements include the expense disclosure where it applies.

Each click goes to a page for one case type with a short screening form. People who submit the form receive an instant text and email confirming their request, and your intake team gets the lead in real time with the answers attached. We do not send unsolicited texts to people who have not asked to be contacted.

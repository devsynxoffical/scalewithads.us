VSL Script Structure: An 8-Part Framework for High-Ticket Offers

The 8-part VSL script structure we use for high-ticket offers, with a fill-in template and tips for shorter VSLs that run as Meta ads.

A video sales letter (VSL) is a sales letter delivered as a video: one continuous argument that takes a viewer from "this is interesting" to "I want to talk to you". Most weak VSLs aren't badly filmed; they're badly structured. This is the 8-part structure our team uses as a starting point for high-ticket offers.

The 8-part VSL script structure

1. Hook

The first seconds decide whether anyone watches. Call out who the video is for and the specific outcome or problem it addresses. A good hook is specific enough that the right viewer feels singled out and the wrong viewer leaves.

2. Problem

Describe the problem the way your buyer would describe it, including what they've already tried. If they recognize their own situation, they'll trust that you understand the solution.

3. Story and credibility

Explain briefly why you're worth listening to: your experience, how you discovered the approach and who you've helped. Keep it short and relevant to the viewer's problem.

4. Unique mechanism

Name and explain the approach that makes your solution different. This answers "why will this work when other things didn't?" and stops the viewer comparing you only on price.

5. Proof

Show evidence that the mechanism works: case studies, client results, screenshots and testimonials. Use only real, verifiable results, and include context so the numbers mean something.

6. Offer

Spell out exactly what the viewer gets, how it works and what happens after they take the next step. Clarity sells better than hype.

7. Risk reversal

Address the biggest reason someone would hesitate. That might be a guarantee, a trial or simply a clear explanation of what happens if it isn't a fit. Only promise what you'll put in writing.

8. Call to action

Tell the viewer exactly what to do next, usually booking a call or applying, and why now is a good time. Any urgency should be real, such as limited onboarding capacity.

Fill-in VSL script template

  1. 1Hook: "If you're a [who] trying to [outcome] without [pain], watch this."
  2. 2Problem: "Most [who] try [common approach], but [why it fails]."
  3. 3Credibility: "We've spent [time] doing [relevant work] for [type of client]."
  4. 4Mechanism: "Instead of [old way], we use [named approach], which [how it works]."
  5. 5Proof: "[Client] used this to [specific, verifiable result]."
  6. 6Offer: "Here's exactly what we do: [deliverables and process]."
  7. 7Risk reversal: "If [condition], then [what you commit to]."
  8. 8CTA: "Click below to [next step]. On the call we'll [what they get]."

How long should a VSL be?

There's no single right length; it depends on price and how much the viewer needs to understand before acting. Higher-priced offers usually need more explanation and proof. Test lengths rather than following a rule. A practical approach is to keep the long VSL on the landing page and cut shorter versions of the hook, mechanism and proof sections to run as Meta ads that send viewers there.

Turning a VSL into Meta ads

  • Cut several hooks from the same VSL and test them as separate ads.
  • Add captions; many people watch with the sound off.
  • Keep claims consistent with what the landing page and offer actually deliver.
  • Send viewers to a page that continues the same argument instead of starting over.

Frequently asked questions

What is a VSL?

A video sales letter: a sales letter delivered as one continuous video that takes the viewer from interest to taking the next step, such as booking a call.

What is the structure of a VSL script?

Hook, problem, story and credibility, unique mechanism, proof, offer, risk reversal and call to action.

How long should a VSL be?

There is no single right length. Higher-priced offers usually need more explanation and proof, so test lengths. A practical approach is a long VSL on the landing page and shorter cut-downs running as ads.

Can you use a VSL as a Meta ad?

Yes. Cut several hooks, the mechanism and proof sections into short captioned ads, and send viewers to a page that continues the same argument.
